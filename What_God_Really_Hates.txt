What God Really Hates

God hates the good man.

Not the murderer. Not the thief. Not the frothing little villain who wakes each morning wondering which commandment might look nicest broken before lunch.

Those men are useful.

They make excellent advertising.

No, what truly endangers God is the decent man who hears the great indictment of humanity and simply says:

“Yeah, no.”

He reads that he is inherently depraved, wicked from birth, capable of every horror, and deserving of death before breakfast.

Then he looks up.

“I am not one of your psycho freaks. I do not feel the compulsion to stab everything in front of me. I do not need divine supervision to keep the knives in the drawer.”

There goes the entire sales pitch.

Christianity begins with a universal insult and calls the cure good news. You are corrupt. Your heart is diseased. Your finest deeds are filthy. Without God, you would apparently be gnawing on your neighbor by Thursday.

Then comes the good man.

He returns the wallet.

He loves his children.

He keeps his word when no one is watching.

He does not do these things because heaven is taking attendance. He does them because other people are real to him. Their pain matters. Their trust matters. Civilization matters.

How irritating.

He is living evidence that goodness may not require a celestial policeman, and worse, that pleasure may not require a permit.

Ask him about some harmless little joy the church spent centuries coating in shame.

“Masturbation?”

He shrugs.

“That is not an addiction. That is a great time.”

Somewhere, a youth pastor drops his clipboard.

Of course, any appetite can become destructive. But pleasure is not guilty merely because it feels good. Pleasure becomes wrong when it creates a victim, not when it fails to produce an apology.

That distinction is fatal to the old machinery.

For what this God appears to hate most is the idea He has labored so long to stamp out of humanity’s collective knowledge:

You may enjoy something wonderful without paying for it in pain.

No blood.

No shame.

No little spiritual invoice waiting beneath the dessert.

The good man eats, laughs, loves, touches, rests, and enjoys his brief possession of a body. Then he wakes the next morning without inventing a courtroom in the sky.

These men are the quiet symbols of civilization, and that is precisely what makes them terrifying. They prove that restraint can come from empathy, that order can arise without threats, and that a person may refuse cruelty simply because cruelty is ugly.

They do not need hell to frighten them into humanity.

They were already human.

But religion has always understood the usefulness of sacrifice. First comes the ancient altar: blood, smoke, obedience, a desert people taught that holiness carries an invoice. Then Christianity performs its magnificent reversal.

No more sacrifices, it announces.

One final sacrifice has already been made.

And now the entire species owes forever.

Wonderful accounting.

The old altar was not abolished. The bill was internationalized. The target is no longer one small nation in the desert. It is me. It is you. It is every child born already holding a receipt for an execution he never requested.

And if we refuse the debt?

Hell.

Very well.

If hell is real, is that not price enough?

If the end is eternal darkness, then paradoxically our short existence demands pleasure. Not cruelty. Not greed. Not appetite that turns another person into an object. Harmless pleasure. Honest pleasure. The kind that leaves no victim and requires no forgiveness.

A good meal.

A lover’s body.

A morning spent doing absolutely nothing useful.

Laughter so loud that no sermon can be heard over it.

If eternity is to be the punishment, why spend the afternoon punishing yourself in advance?

So stop feeling guilty.

There is no God.

And if there is?

Let hell be price enough.

No more advance payments.

No more blood upon the altar of imagined debt.

Sacrifice no more.
