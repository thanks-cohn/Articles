CUT IT OFF FOR THE LORD

Wouldn’t You?

The modern church has discovered the sanctity of the penis.

It was a late discovery, but let us be grateful.

To hear certain Christians tell it, the modern world is roaming the streets with a clipboard and a pair of garden shears, forcing grown men to surrender perfectly innocent anatomy to the great secular machine.

Let us be clear before anyone becomes excitable.

This is not an advertisement for cutting anything off. No one is being encouraged to injure himself. What a consenting adult decides with his doctors is his concern, and there are enough real medical discussions to be had without a satirist reaching for the scalpel.

But that is not the joke.

The joke is the church.

Because the same institution now clutching its pearls over the terrible idea of a man permanently altering his body has spent centuries teaching that the highest form of love is complete bodily surrender to God.

Your comfort.

Your pleasure.

Your freedom.

Your family.

Your blood.

Your life.

Everything.

Well, almost everything.

Apparently one organ has negotiated diplomatic immunity.

So let us accept the church’s language for a moment. Let us call the operation horrifying. Let us call it mutilation. Let us grant every trembling adjective the pulpit demands.

Now I have one small question.

If God asked you to do it, wouldn’t you?

No?

Do you not love Him?

There it is.

That is the question hiding beneath all the outrage.

Not whether anyone should do such a thing. They should not do it for a sermon, a threat, a verse, or some pastor’s approval. The question is whether the truly devout believer could refuse if the demand came stamped with heaven’s authority.

Imagine the announcement next Sunday.

“My beloved congregation, the Lord requires a sacrifice.”

A nervous silence.

“An intimate sacrifice.”

A considerably more nervous silence.

Would the church still call it mutilation?

Of course not.

By Monday it would be a covenant.

By Tuesday it would be consecration.

By Wednesday there would be a men’s retreat, three worship songs, and a testimonial from someone insisting he had never felt more complete.

The knife would not have changed.

The wound would not have changed.

Only the owner of the altar.

That is what makes the outrage so funny.

Christianity does not possess some ancient, inviolable doctrine that the male body must remain precisely as nature delivered it. Biblical religion introduced itself to generations of men with circumcision, a permanent alteration of genital flesh commanded as a sign of belonging to God.

Only the foreskin, naturally.

The Lord was pacing Himself.

Then Jesus spoke of cutting off hands, plucking out eyes, and men making themselves eunuchs for the kingdom of heaven. The church will rush to explain that some of this is metaphor.

Wonderful.

But metaphors reveal values, and this particular collection is not exactly a celebration of bodily inviolability.

The message is unmistakable.

Nothing is too precious to surrender if God requires it.

Not your hand.

Not your eye.

Not your sexuality.

Not your life.

And if you hesitate, Christianity has a remarkably efficient little question waiting for you.

Do you love God more than yourself?

Careful.

A wrong answer can last forever.

That is why this is not an argument for mutilation. It is an argument about obedience. More precisely, it is an argument about an institution that condemns bodily sacrifice when an adult chooses it for himself, yet praises sacrifice as the crown of devotion when God, scripture, or clergy claims the body first.

The church does not say, “Your body is absolutely yours and no authority may demand a piece of it.”

It cannot say that.

Its theology depends upon the opposite.

Your body belongs to God.

Your life belongs to God.

You were bought with a price.

You are a living sacrifice.

All that remains is to determine what the Owner wants done with His property.

So when believers recoil and cry, “No loving God would ever ask that,” they have quietly changed the subject.

I did not ask whether He would.

I asked what you would do if He did.

Would you refuse?

Then there is something you love more than God.

Would you obey?

Then bodily alteration was never inherently beyond the pale. It merely required the proper authorization.

And what if the command did not arrive in thunder?

What if it came the usual way?

Through a pastor.

Through a council.

Through a sacred text.

Through a room full of solemn men insisting they had prayed very carefully and that resistance was merely pride wearing the costume of prudence.

Would you still laugh?

Would you walk out?

Or would the old machinery begin to turn?

Abraham obeyed.

The martyrs obeyed.

Christ gave His body.

Do you think you are better than they were?

Don’t you love God?

There is the blade.

Not steel.

Guilt.

The church has rarely needed to hold people down when it can teach them that refusal is selfishness, pain is holiness, and terror is the Holy Spirit pressing upon the heart.

That is the hypocrisy worth mocking.

Not transgender people.

Not consenting adults making decisions the church dislikes.

The target is the believer who points at another person’s altered body and declares, with magnificent confidence, “I would never,” while singing every Sunday that he would surrender absolutely anything his Lord requested.

Anything?

Are you sure?

Because if the church requested the very sacrifice it condemns today, it would not call the act mutilation tomorrow.

It would call it faith.

It would polish the knife.

It would bless the room.

It would praise the first man brave enough to obey.

And when you backed toward the door, pale and suddenly protective of the flesh God supposedly owns, someone would place a gentle hand upon your shoulder and whisper the only argument they ever really needed:

Wouldn’t you?

Don’t you love Him?
